# Importance of Cybersecurity Training

Cybersecurity skills are developed through **practice and continuous training**.

> **Nobody becomes a cybersecurity expert without practice.**

Cybersecurity professionals must also keep their skills updated because:

* New vulnerabilities are discovered.
* New attack techniques appear.
* Security tools continuously change.

Training should be done in a **safe lab environment** instead of production systems.

For example, platforms such as [TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) allow you to learn and practice attacks safely.

## Why is Training Important?

Training prepares the security team **before a real cyber attack happens**.

Instead of learning a tool during an active incident, the team already knows:

* What to do.
* Which tools to use.
* How to investigate.
* How to respond.

This helps reduce mistakes and respond faster.

Training also improves the skills of existing employees, so an organization can **increase the team's capability without always hiring more people**.

Junior employees can learn faster through structured training, and senior employees don't need to repeatedly teach the same basic concepts.

## Centralized Training

**Centralized training** gives everyone in the organization a common learning path and skill baseline.

It helps organizations understand:

* What skills an employee has.
* What skills they need to improve.
* Which tasks they can handle.
* What they need to learn for the next role.

## CTF

A **CTF (Capture the Flag)** is a cybersecurity challenge where individuals or teams solve security problems and find hidden flags.

CTFs help improve:

* Practical skills.
* Problem-solving.
* Teamwork.
* Communication.

# Financial Value of Cybersecurity Training

Cybersecurity training is an **investment in employees**.

The main idea is simple:

> **Training improves employee productivity and can save the company money.**

### Example

A cybersecurity team has **10 employees**.

Each employee costs the company **$80,000 per year**.

Training improves productivity by **4%**.

The financial gain is:

```text
10 × 4% × $80,000 = $32,000
```

So, the company gains **$32,000 in productivity value**.

If training costs **$500 per employee**:

```text
10 × $500 = $5,000
```

Total training cost = **$5,000**.

### ROI (Return on Investment)

**ROI** shows the financial benefit received compared with the money invested.

```text
ROI = Gain ÷ Cost
```

For this example:

```text
$32,000 ÷ $5,000 = 6.4

6.4 × 100 = 640%
```

**ROI = 640%**

This means the value gained from training is **6.4 times the training cost**.

### Training Proposal

A **training proposal** is a document used to convince management to provide a budget for employee training.

It can include:

* Training cost.
* Expected productivity improvement.
* Financial savings.
* ROI.
* Benefits to the cybersecurity team.

### Quick Revision

> **Training → Better productivity → Financial gain**

> **ROI = Gain ÷ Investment × 100**

In the example: **$5,000 training cost → $32,000 productivity gain → 640% ROI.**
